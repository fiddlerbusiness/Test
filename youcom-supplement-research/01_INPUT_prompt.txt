### 4. OUTCOME DEFINITIONS & MEASUREMENT RULES
PILLAR 1 (STRENGTH): 1RM or isometric/isokinetic maximal strength (kg and %); hypertrophy measured by ultrasound, MRI/CT cross-sectional area, or fiber CSA, kept separate from DXA/BIA lean mass (lean mass includes intracellular water, which matters especially for creatine); power (countermovement jump, Wingate peak/mean power); repetitions to failure and volume load; recovery (force recovery, CK, DOMS). Separate ACUTE (single dose) from CHRONIC (training study) effects.

PILLAR 2 (ENDURANCE): VO2max (ml/kg/min and L/min), lactate threshold, critical power, exercise economy, time-trial performance, time-to-exhaustion. Time-to-exhaustion produces much larger percentage changes than time-trial tests (Hopkins, Hawley & Burke, Med Sci Sports Exerc, 1999); report the two test types separately and never merge their percentages. Prefer time-trial data for practical relevance.

PILLAR 3 (TESTOSTERONE):
- Report total testosterone in BOTH ng/dL and nmol/L (1 nmol/L = 28.84 ng/dL).
- Free testosterone: state whether measured by equilibrium dialysis or calculated (e.g., Vermeulen equation; Vermeulen et al., J Clin Endocrinol Metab, 1999). Treat salivary testosterone and direct analog free-T immunoassays as lower-quality evidence.
- Record assay method (LC-MS/MS vs immunoassay), sampling time (morning, fasting), and number of samples (Rosner et al., Endocrine Society position statement, J Clin Endocrinol Metab, 2007).
- Also extract LH, FSH, SHBG, estradiol, cortisol (serum vs salivary), and the testosterone:cortisol ratio when reported.
- PHYSIOLOGICAL RELEVANCE CHECK (mandatory): For every testosterone change, judge (i) whether it exceeds normal diurnal and day-to-day variability, (ii) where it sits relative to the harmonized reference range for non-obese men aged 19-39 (Travison et al., J Clin Endocrinol Metab, 2017; verify the exact range in the source), and (iii) whether a change of that magnitude within the physiological range plausibly affects strength or hypertrophy. Use the dose-response data of Bhasin et al. (Am J Physiol Endocrinol Metab, 2001) and the training studies of West & Phillips (Eur J Appl Physiol, 2012) and Morton et al. (J Appl Physiol, 2016) as anchors. State plainly when a "statistically significant" increase is practically irrelevant.

### 7. EVIDENCE RULES (non-negotiable)
7.1 HIERARCHY: (1) systematic reviews/meta-analyses of RCTs (prefer recent, pre-registered, GRADE-rated) and umbrella reviews (check for overlap of primary studies) > (2) individual double-blind placebo-controlled RCTs (crossover designs acceptable for acute effects) > (3) non-randomized or observational human studies > (4) mechanistic human studies > (5) animal/in-vitro studies.

7.2 META-ANALYSIS APPRAISAL: For each meta-analysis report k (studies), n (participants), effect metric, point estimate, 95% CI, heterogeneity (I2), prediction interval if reported, risk-of-bias assessment, publication-bias tests, and GRADE certainty if reported. Flag meta-analyses that pool incompatible populations (hypogonadal with eugonadal, men with women, young with old, clinical with healthy). Do not double-count overlapping meta-analyses. When meta-analyses conflict, explain WHY (inclusion criteria, populations, outcome definitions, analytic choices) and state which estimate is more credible.

7.3 SOURCE RULES: Cite ONLY peer-reviewed primary literature, systematic reviews, official position/consensus statements, and documents from regulatory or public-health agencies (EFSA, NIH ODS, FDA, BfR, RIVM, ANSES, DTU, AIS, WADA). NEVER cite supplement manufacturers or retailers, fitness magazines or blogs, influencer podcasts or websites, forums, social media, press releases, trade/industry news, or AI-generated content. Industry-funded trials may be cited but MUST be flagged; this is critical for branded botanical extracts.

7.4 QUANTIFICATION (mandatory): For every efficacy claim report the effect metric (SMD/Hedges' g, MD/WMD in natural units, or % change), point estimate, 95% CI, k, n, intervention duration, and dose. Natural units: strength in kg and %, VO2max in ml/kg/min and %, time-trial performance in % time or power, testosterone in ng/dL and nmol/L. If a source reports only p-values, write "effect size not reported"; compute an effect size only if the data allow and label it "calculated by reviewer". Prefer absolute changes over relative changes.

7.5 PRACTICAL RELEVANCE: Compare effects with measurement error, day-to-day variability, and the smallest worthwhile change. Statistical significance is not practical relevance.

7.6 EVIDENCE TIERS (use these exact labels, assigned PER PILLAR):
- "Tier 1: Established / Replicated" - at least one high-quality meta-analysis of RCTs or several consistent RCTs in a relevant population; consistent direction; practically meaningful effect; plausible mechanism; acceptable safety.
- "Tier 2: Preliminary / Context-Dependent" - positive but limited, heterogeneous, low-certainty or industry-dominated evidence; OR benefit restricted to a subgroup (deficient, hypogonadal, untrained, specific exercise domain); OR small effect of uncertain practical relevance.
- "Tier 3: Ineffective / Debunked / Marketing Hype" - consistent null human RCTs; OR only animal/in-vitro/mechanistic/observational support; OR unfavorable risk-benefit ratio.
A supplement can be Tier 1 for one pillar and Tier 3 for another. Assign a SEPARATE safety flag (low / moderate / high concern) independent of efficacy.

7.7 TESTOSTERONE SPLIT (mandatory): For every agent with testosterone claims give TWO separate verdicts: (A) correction of deficiency or hypogonadal/low-normal state; (B) effect in replete, eugonadal, healthy men aged 20-40. Never transfer (A) to (B). If no RCT data exist for (B), write exactly: "No RCT data in eugonadal men aged 20-40."

7.8 COUNTER-EVIDENCE (mandatory): For every Tier 1 and Tier 2 item, state the strongest null or negative evidence and the main objections (funding, small n, short duration, surrogate outcomes, unblinding through side effects such as beta-alanine paresthesia or caffeine stimulation, selective reporting). No artificial balance: if the evidence is clear, say so.

7.9 ZERO FABRICATION RULE: Never invent studies, authors, journals, years, DOIs, PMIDs, numbers, or quotations. Every number must be traceable to a cited source. If a value cannot be found, use null or "not reported". If a bibliographic detail cannot be verified, set "verified": false for that source. Never guess.

7.10 RECENCY: Prefer the most recent high-quality synthesis, but keep landmark trials when they define the evidence. Mark preprints as preprints. State the publication year for every source.

7.11 SAFETY: For every lever report adverse effects, tolerable upper intake levels (EFSA and US values where they differ), drug interactions, contraindications, regulatory warnings or bans, contamination/adulteration risk, and anti-doping considerations.
