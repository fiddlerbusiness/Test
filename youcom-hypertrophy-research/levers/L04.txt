RUN: L04 - Per-session volume ceiling ("junk volume")
LEVER: sets per muscle per session. Question: is there a point of diminishing returns or an upper threshold beyond which extra sets add nothing or impair growth?
Do NOT assume a ceiling exists - test it. Per-session volume is inseparable from weekly volume and frequency: analyse that interaction explicitly (for this lever only, weekly volume and frequency are in scope).
Questions:
a) Shape of the per-session dose-response; any fitted threshold with its uncertainty; how many studies/effect sizes exist at high per-session volumes (e.g., >10 sets per muscle per session).
b) Direct vs fractional (indirect) set counting and how the counting rule changes the result.
c) Interaction with weekly volume and frequency (same weekly volume split across more sessions).
d) Untrained vs trained.
e) Confounding of early post-training hypertrophy measures by muscle damage/oedema at high volumes.
Required checks:
(1) Compare the per-session meta-regressions of Remmert et al. (SportRxiv preprint 2025) with the weekly dose-response meta-regressions of Pelland et al. (Sports Medicine; SportRxiv preprint 2024) and with Krieger's unpublished 2017 per-session meta-regression (cite the latter only as reported by others, never as a primary source).
(2) Report the retraction status of studies previously used to argue for volume ceilings and exclude retracted work from the evidence.
Leads to verify (not pre-approved conclusions):
- Remmert J, Pelland J, Robinson Z, Hinson S, Zourdos M, "Meta-regressions of the effect of per-session volume on hypertrophy and strength" (SportRxiv preprint 2025; check exact title and journal publication).
- Pelland JC, Remmert JF, Robinson ZP, Hinson SR, Zourdos MC, "The resistance training dose response: meta-regressions exploring the effects of weekly volume and frequency on muscle hypertrophy and strength gains" (Sports Medicine 2025/2026; SportRxiv preprint 2024).
- Schoenfeld BJ, Ogborn D, Krieger JW (2017, Journal of Sports Sciences) - weekly volume dose-response.
- Schoenfeld BJ et al. (2019, Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise) - volume in trained men.
- Baz-Valle E et al. (2022, Journal of Human Kinetics) - systematic review of training volumes.
- Brigatto FA et al. (2019, Journal of Strength and Conditioning Research) - equal weekly volume split across sessions.
- Damas F et al. (2016, Journal of Physiology) and Damas F et al. (2016, European Journal of Applied Physiology) - muscle damage and oedema confounding early hypertrophy measures.
- Damas F et al. (2019, Journal of Applied Physiology) - individual responses to changing training variables in trained men.
- Retraction check: Barbalho M et al., "Evidence of a ceiling effect for training volume in muscle hypertrophy and strength in trained men - less is more?" (International Journal of Sports Physiology and Performance; reportedly retracted April 2020) and related Barbalho et al. papers flagged for improbable data (incl. the trained-women volume study in Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise 2019) - determine the current status of each.
